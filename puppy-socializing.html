🐾 Why early matters

The first three months.

The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior puts the primary socialization window in a puppy's first three months. Your puppy is building a reference list of what counts as normal — and whatever doesn't make the list in time tends to get filed later as a threat.

A puppy who has met dogs of different sizes, walked on new surfaces, heard a busy room and been handled by new people is far less likely to become the adult who lunges at the vet or panics at the groomer. That's why our yards are sanitized and we set health requirements before day one.

We encourage positive training: praise, affection, healthy treats and lots of repetition. Puppies have short attention spans and need calm interactions and structured rest — patience and love are key.

🐾 Health requirements

Before day one.

🧪

Fecal sample

Puppies under 6 months need proof of a clear fecal exam from a vet showing no parasites.

💉

Parvo / Distemper

At least one set of vaccinations given 7+ days before the pre-screening day, kept up to date. Distemper titers accepted for dogs over 1 year.

🩺

Rabies

Required for all dogs except puppies under 4 months. Maricopa County requires rabies and a license at 3 months.

🤧

Bordetella

Recommended for puppies whose immune systems are still developing — but optional. You choose what's right for your dog.

✂️

Spay / neuter

Required by 2 years of age — more vets recommend waiting so bodies can develop, and we agree.
